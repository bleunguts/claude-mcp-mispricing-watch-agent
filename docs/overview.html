The problem

A wrong rate goes public fast

  • The bank publishes rates. They flow straight to the public and to Bloomberg.
  • A price far from where it was an hour ago can still be right. Markets move.
  • The agent raises an alarm when the pricing system itself looks broken. A human then checks.
Win condition: few alarms, almost all real.

How the agent decides

Six rules, no magic number

Judge the gap

Compare the price with a benchmark. Market moves hit both, so they cancel out.

Own normal per node

Each tenor learns its normal from its own history, 1 month up to 1 year. The 10Y and 50Y get different tolerances.

Must persist

One odd tick is ignored. The gap has to hold.

One cause, one alarm

Ten nodes broken by the same thing is a single alarm.

Budget first

Pick how many false alarms you can live with, then derive the tolerance.

Second opinion from history

Is the price outside the middle 90% of the node's own 1W, 1M or 3M? Also the fallback when a node has no benchmark, like a 3W tenor.

A miss is catastrophic for the engineer, but traders usually catch it. So it must be rare. These targets will evolve.

Fake world

  • One small curve, about a month of history
  • A benchmark series, and one node with none
  • Faults planted at known times: stale, unit error, setting change
  • Real market moves that must stay quiet

Five tools

  • get_history
  • get_benchmark
  • compute_spread_stats
  • compute_history_outlier
  • check_stale

Pass mark

  • At least 80% of alarms are real
  • At most 5% of faults missed
  • Beats a plain fixed-threshold rule

Left out on purpose

  • Change log tool
  • Wire price
  • Cause labels
  • Futures and swaps seam
  • Several curves
  • Web benchmarks, Excel report

Output is a plain alarm or no alarm. You may view it later in Grafana or the .NET Aspire dashboard (to be chosen).
